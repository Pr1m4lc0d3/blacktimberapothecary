Behind the blend

How this one was made

Herbal wellness tea has a reputation, and it's mostly earned: it tends to taste like something you finish out of duty. Buckshot & Gunpowder was put together the other way around. The herbs were chosen for what they do. Then a great deal of care went into making the thirteen of them actually taste good together.

The unusual part

That balancing was worked out in conversation with the Apothecary — a specialist artificial intelligence built to work from a library of herbal literature rather than from thin air. Not a search engine and not a chatbot: something narrow, grounded in specific texts, and good at one question — how do these herbs sit with one another?

The conversation was about proportion and harmony. Which roots dominate and which get buried. Where the bitterness lands. What the rooibos does to the gunpowder green. Why orange peel at a quarter part and not a half. It was a long back-and-forth about flavor, and it ran until the ratios stopped changing.

Then the herbs were weighed out, mixed by hand, and brewed. That's the part no software does. It turned out delicious, so it became the recipe.

What it did, and what it didn't

What it did

  • Worked through flavor balance and how the thirteen herbs harmonize.
  • Helped settle the proportions — the parts, and what they add up to.
  • Stores the finished recipe, and does the batch math, costing and sourcing for every production run.

What it didn't

  • It didn't decide what should be good for you. A person chose the herbs and what they're for.
  • It didn't perform a medical or safety review, and nothing here is a health claim — see the notice at the foot of this page.
  • It didn't taste anything. Every batch is judged by a human mouth before it goes in a box.

Why say so at all

Because it's true, and because a food made with this kind of help is new enough that quietly leaving it out would be its own kind of answer. The honest version is better than the vague one: a machine that had read the literature helped make a tea taste good, and a person in Montana made the tea.

The Apothecary runs inside CODE·A.I., software built by the same one-man shop that blends the tea — deliberon.com.
